# Documentation and Presentation

Module 5 of the CRE[AT]E Course. Covers how to document a project's design process and present it effectively for design reviews and the Final Event.

## Why documentation matters

A large part of the CRE[AT]E Challenge is documenting and presenting your work. Since the Challenge is distributed, the only way most remote mentors, staff, and other students will ever see a project is through the design reviews and documents submitted. Effective communication helps with:

- letting remote mentors understand what a team is doing in order to provide better feedback
- justifying funding requests to Beaver Works, or funding pitches to other potential sponsors
- preserving the process for others who may want to make something similar in the future (dramatically expanding impact)

## Tips for Design Review Videos

Common problems and solutions seen over the years when teams submit design reviews:

- Cameras should be on while presenting — "we want to see your team!"
- Watch for team faces blocking important slide content; reposition the camera if needed.
- Zoom is recommended for recording (multiple locations, no watermarks).
- Optional: [OBS (Open Broadcaster Software)](https://obsproject.com/) to fine-tune camera/presentation placement.
- Check audio — "we cannot review what we cannot hear."

## Documentation and Presentation Review (assignment)

1. Review the final submission requirements under the "Black Gate" heading (links to Design Reviews page).
2. Each team member picks a different team from a previous season (2022-2023 or 2023-2024 Past Projects).
   - At least one member should pick a team that won the Documentation Award.
   - Don't choose your own team if you were on one of the previous-season teams.
3. Watch that team's final presentation video (from the Presentation Playlist) and read their final document.
4. Answer, separately for the video and the document:
   - What did that team do well (per submission requirements)? (Note: time allowances for videos differed slightly between seasons.)
   - What could have been improved?
   - How would elements of the video or document apply to your own project, if known?

Note: this review is of *final* submissions, so they'll look more polished than a team's own early design reviews — that's expected.
