Maker Skills

Designing and fabricating assistive technology projects can involve a wide variety of skills depending on what you are building. From mechanical engineering and 3D printing to web development, electronics, and sewing — AT projects have a lot in common with the Maker Movement.

This module is divided into four sections covering the most common technical domains in CRE[AT]E projects. Please complete two sections that are most relevant to your project.

Note

If you find that your project covers something quite different from what is in these sections, and you would like additional resources, please ask on the Discord!

Skill Sections

Choose two sections most relevant to your project

Each section focuses on a different technical domain. Read the descriptions below to decide which two best match the direction of your team's project.

🔩
Mechanical Making

Physical fabrication techniques for designing and building AT devices. Covers machine tools and shops (laser cutters, drill presses, mills), 3D printing and FDM fundamentals, and computer-aided design using Onshape — BWSI AT's recommended free, browser-based CAD platform.

Mechanical Making →
💻
Software

Programming for web, desktop, mobile, or microcontroller-based projects. Covers version control with GitHub, UI design with Figma, web and mobile development (including MIT App Inventor for Android), and AI and machine learning tools — with guidance on accessibility and data bias for AT applications.

Software →
⚡
Electronics

Circuits, microcontrollers, and sensors for devices that can sense the world and respond dynamically. Introduces electrical engineering principles via Khan Academy, popular microcontroller platforms (Arduino, Adafruit Feather, Raspberry Pi), and hands-on circuit simulation with Tinkercad before investing in hardware.

Electronics →
🧤
Wearables

Technology worn on the body — from body-mounted sensors to electronics embedded in clothing or mechanical braces. Covers electronic components suited to wearables, key design considerations (ergonomics, measuring for fit, hygiene, aesthetics), and the AFFOA wearable technology curriculum for hands-on prototyping.

Wearables →
