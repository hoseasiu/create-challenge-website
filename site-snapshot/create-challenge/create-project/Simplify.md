# Simplify

The fifth step of the CRE[AT]E Project section, between Scoping a Project and Design Reviews. Encourages teams to reduce complexity to the essential need — simpler designs are often more reliable and easier to build, test, and use. No Previous/Next module navigation on this page (matching its siblings Scoping a Project and Design Reviews).

## Overview / Why simplify?

Opens with a tongue-in-cheek story about a wildly over-engineered, over-complicated hypothetical project that failed for unclear reasons, then makes the point that real AT products have failed (or underperformed) from excess complexity. Lists the benefits of simplifying: easier/cheaper production, less lead time on parts, faster iteration, less debugging/testing, easier reproducibility.

## Case Study: Chase's Watering Can

Describes Team Vextraordinary's 2023–2024 project for Chase, a co-designer who loves to garden but has limited hand/arm movement — the team started with a complex device concept and simplified it. Four reflection questions are given to guide reading. The page then links out to the team's design document via a Google Sites auto-generated rich link-preview card (title + description + an auto-fetched thumbnail image from a signed, non-durable `lh3.googleusercontent.com` URL) pointing to `/create-challenge/2024-projects-section/2024-project-page-vextraordinary` (the 2024 Past Projects page for Team Vextraordinary — itself not yet a local HTML embed).

## How can you simplify your design?

Two assignments:
- **Constrained Brainstorming** — 8 "what if" prompts removing a constraint (internet, motors, electricity, a fabrication tool, half a primary material, half the size, half the budget, plus two of your own), asking how each modification would affect the product.
- **Red Teaming** — a "time-travel to the postmortem" thought exercise: imagine the current design failed weeks from now, work out why, then bring back lessons to apply now.

Closes with a note that teams aren't required to implement any of the considered simplifications, but should write down the risk taken on (and justification) for any simpler design they chose not to pursue.
