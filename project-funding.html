Project Funding

How to request funding for your project

Registered teams who need money to build their assistive technology (AT) product can request funding directly from Beaver Works by email. This is the single, canonical process for project funding requests — it replaces any other description of funding you may have seen on other pages of this site.

Teams may receive up to $125 of funding from Beaver Works. At the moment, funding is only available to US-based high school teams.

No fixed deadline is currently published for funding requests. If your team is planning around a specific date, reach out to bwsi-create-challenge@mit.edu to confirm timing before you count on funding arriving by a certain point.

The process

Email us to request funding

To request funding, send an email to bwsi-create-challenge@mit.edu with the following:

  1. Subject line: TEAM NUMBER - funding request, replacing TEAM NUMBER with your team's 3- or 4-digit team number.
  2. Cc: all team members on the email.
  3. Body: an explanation of what you need funding for. The more detail you provide, the easier it is for us to make a determination.

That's the whole mechanism — there is no separate funding form, and funding is not requested or granted as part of a mentor design review.

Prioritization

How funding requests are prioritized

Funding is limited, so requests are prioritized. Beaver Works uses the financial background information your team supplied during registration to help decide which requests to prioritize. Providing clear, specific detail in your request email (what the funds are for and why they're needed) also helps us evaluate it quickly.

Example

A worked example of a good funding request

This is a sample email for illustration only — it is not a real team's data.

To: bwsi-create-challenge@mit.edu
Cc: jordan.lee@example.com, priya.raman@example.com, sam.okafor@example.com
Subject: 214 - funding request
Hello, Our team (214) is requesting funding to purchase parts for our co-designer's adaptive utensil grip project. We need: - A small servo motor and driver board (~$18) - A roll of flexible TPU filament for 3D printing the grip housing (~$32) - A pack of silicone grip padding material (~$15) Our co-designer has limited hand strength and needs a utensil handle that adjusts grip pressure automatically. We prototyped an initial version with cardboard and a hobby servo we already had, and it worked well enough that we'd like to build a more durable version to test with our co-designer over the next few weeks. Our school's maker space doesn't stock these parts, so we're asking to purchase them ourselves. Total requested: $65 Thank you for considering our request! Team 214
