# Idea to Ink

Module 3 of the CRE[AT]E Course. Covers translating ideas into documented designs: storyboarding, sketching (physical products and software/UI), and digital drawing (raster vs. vector).

## Page Structure

### Section 1 — Module overview (white)
Label: "Module 3 · CRE[AT]E Course" | Heading: "Idea to Ink"

Intro on why drawing/visual communication matters before building. Encourages starting with sketches rather than jumping straight to CAD (SolidWorks, Inventor, Fusion 360). Leads into storyboarding as a starting point.

### Section 2 — Storyboarding: Communicating User Interactions (gray)
Label: "Communicating Your Idea"

Storyboarding described as comic-book-style panels depicting the user's full interaction with the product, start to finish. Emphasizes doing this with/reviewing with the co-designer and other stakeholders. Guidance: list all user actions, then sketch 3–10 panels — enough to force laying out steps, not so many that focus shifts from the big picture.

Resource links:
- "Half the Blank Page: Storyboarding for Product Design" by Cheryl Platz — https://medium.com/ideaplatz/half-the-blank-page-storyboarding-for-product-design-8f92d47fc588

### Section 3 — Drawing for Physical Products (white)
Label: "Drawing & Sketching Your Solution"

Line drawings → shading/perspective for depth → color for materials, optionally on separate layers if working digitally. Notes the importance of sketches showing the product's physical relationship to the user's body for AT products specifically (fit, orientation, relative sizing). Addresses "I suck at drawing" — drawing as a practiceable skill, and the value of its flexibility over CAD early in the process. Notes CAD is still useful later, once decisions need to be pinned down.

Embedded video: "Sketching Tutorial 02 - Industrial Design Sketching Confidence" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bjEw-mulcjA

### Section 4 — Drawing for Software and User Interfaces (gray)
Label: "Drawing & Sketching Your Solution"

Introduces paper prototypes for software/UI design — hand-drawn "screens" used to work out workflow, look/feel, and layout, and to support early user testing. Notes progression from paper to digital tools (Figma, Canva, Adobe XD, PowerPoint).

Tips list for paper prototyping: cover all interactions, size to scale, skip visual polish early, stay flexible, use a "paper flipper" + "facilitator" role during testing with a co-designer, iterate before going digital.

Embedded video: Nielsen Norman Group user-testing demo with paper prototypes (starts at 9:58) — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OlbdIXLunt4&t=598s

### Section 5 — Digital Drawing Details (white)
Label: "Sketch to Digital"

Raster vs. vector art explained. Raster: pixel-based, DPI, .jpg/.png, tools like Photoshop/Procreate/Gimp/Paint. Vector: equation-based lines/shapes, more scalable, preferred for CAD/laser cutting/3D printing workflows, .svg/.pdf/.eps/.ai, tools like Illustrator/Inkscape. Both can feed directly into laser cutter output.
