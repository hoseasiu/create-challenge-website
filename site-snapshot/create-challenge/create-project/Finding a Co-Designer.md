# Finding a Co-Designer

Guidance for CRE[AT]E Project teams on who can be a co-designer and how to find one.

## Who can be a co-designer?

- A co-designer is a community member with a disability or diagnosis presenting a daily-living challenge, who is willing and able to work with the team; co-designers may also be family members, caretakers, or teachers of people with disabilities.
- Co-designers should be a specific person (e.g. "Tony, who has mobility difficulties from cerebral palsy"), not an abstract class of people ("people with mobility issues").
- Co-designers should be geographically co-located with the team whenever possible for in-person testing; working on physical products with co-designers teams cannot test with in person is strongly discouraged (software may be an exception, though in-person testing is still preferred).

## If you need to find a co-designer

- **Personal connections** — ask family, friends, or teachers about people they know, past or present, with a disability or impairment; pay attention to conditions that might not be obviously visible (e.g. elderly neighbors/relatives who benefit from assistive technologies).
- **Local businesses** — hospitals, physical therapists, wheelchair stores, occupational therapists, private special educators, schools, disability lawyers, etc.
- **City/state/non-profit/advocacy resources** — e.g. the Massachusetts Commission for the Blind; get creative.
- Brainstorm several possibilities before reaching out, since the first contact may not work out; consider logistical ease of working with a given person.
- Beaver Works does not match co-designers to teams — finding one is each team's own responsibility.

## Assignment: Find a Co-Designer

If you already have a co-designer, you're done — otherwise, follow the guidance above to find one; a co-designer is required to continue in the CRE[AT]E Challenge. Once found, proceed to the next section, [[Co-Designer Interviews]], to prepare for and conduct an interview.
